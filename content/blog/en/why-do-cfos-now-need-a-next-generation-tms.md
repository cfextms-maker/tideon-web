**Why do CFOs now need a next-generation TMS?**

There was a time when the primary function of a TMS was quite straightforward: Where is the cash? How much money do we have in which bank? What is the status of payments? What does the cash flow look like? Today, however, the challenge facing the CFO is far more complex. It involves multiple banks and accounts, various corporate entities and currencies, rising financing costs, imbalances between collections and payments, currency risk, credit limits, and constantly shifting cash positions. Consequently, the CFO’s question is no longer simply, "How much cash do we have?" The core question is increasingly shifting to: "How much cash will we need, why will we need it, and what should we do about it?" This is where the next-generation TMS comes into play. While a traditional TMS primarily collects, consolidates, and reports data, a next-generation TMS adds an intelligence layer on top of that data. It integrates bank and ERP data, provides visibility into cash positions, forecasts cash flow, highlights variances between actuals and forecasts, and identifies critical issues regarding financing, payments, collections, and risk. In this context, AI is driving a significant transformation within the TMS. The goal of AI is not to make decisions on behalf of the CFO, but to help them quickly identify what matters most amidst hundreds of lines of data. For instance:

• "What is the primary reason for the cash flow variance this month?"

• "What liquidity-related issues require attention over the next 30 days?"

• "Which collections are overdue?"

• "Why did actual cash flow fall short of the forecast?"

• "What notable changes are occurring in financing costs?"

Thus, the role of the TMS is evolving. Old approach: ***Data → Report → CFO → Decision. New approach: Data → Intelligence → Insight → CFO → Decision***. In my opinion, this is where the real difference of next-generation TMS lies. It is not about generating more screens or reports; it is about enabling the CFO to move more quickly from the question "What happened?" to "Why did it happen?", "What will happen?", and "What should I pay attention to?"
